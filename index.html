Istanbul Metrobüs Explained: Route, Stops, Fares and How It Works

A plain, factual overview of the bus rapid transit line that links Istanbul's European and Asian sides.

Key facts

  • Type: Bus rapid transit (BRT) on a dedicated lane
  • Operator: İETT, Istanbul's public bus authority
  • Route: Beylikdüzü (Europe) to Söğütlüçeşme (Asia), about 50 km
  • Stops: 44
  • Payment: Istanbulkart
  • Bosphorus crossing: 15 July Martyrs Bridge

What the Metrobüs is

The Metrobüs is a bus rapid transit line in Istanbul. Instead of sharing ordinary roads with cars, its buses run in a separate lane in the middle of the main highway. Passengers board at stations built on this central strip, and most stations are reached by a footbridge or an underpass.

The line is one of the longest of its kind in the world. It starts in the western district of Beylikdüzü, runs east across the European side, crosses the Bosphorus, and ends at Söğütlüçeşme in Kadıköy on the Asian side. Because it joins districts on both continents, it is used daily by commuters, students, and visitors.

A short history

The Metrobüs opened in September 2007 with a first section on the European side between Topkapı and Zeytinburnu. The line was extended in stages over the following years. In 2008 it was extended across the Bosphorus to the Asian side, and a later extension reached Beylikdüzü in the west, which gave the line the length it has today.

The bridge it uses was formerly called the Bosphorus Bridge and is now officially named the 15 July Martyrs Bridge. The bus lane over the bridge is part of the same dedicated system.

How the system works

Dedicated lane

The main design feature is the separated lane. Because other vehicles cannot enter it, the buses are less affected by general road congestion. This helps the line keep a more predictable travel time than ordinary bus routes.

Vehicles

The line uses long articulated buses with several wide doors. The doors open at platform level, which makes boarding and leaving faster. The vehicles are designed to carry a large number of passengers at once.

Stations and turnstiles

Passengers pass through a turnstile before reaching the platform. This means fares are paid before boarding, not on the bus, and it speeds up the time spent at each stop.

Service pattern

Buses run frequently during the day, with the highest frequency in the morning and evening peak periods. The line also provides service late at night with fewer departures. Exact timetables can change, so the operator's official sources are the best reference for current schedules.

Payment

Fares are paid with the Istanbulkart, the rechargeable smart card used across public transport in Istanbul. The same card works on buses, metro, trams, ferries, Marmaray, and the Metrobüs. Cash is not used at the turnstiles.

  • Cards are sold at ticket machines and authorized kiosks.
  • Balance can be added at top-up machines at many stations.
  • The fare is deducted when the card is tapped at the turnstile.
  • Transfers made within the allowed time on the same card are charged at a reduced rate under the city's integrated fare rules.
  • Discounted and free categories exist for certain groups, such as students and older residents, under official conditions.

Fare amounts are updated from time to time. The current price is shown at stations and on the official transport authority's channels.

Using the line step by step

  1. Walk to the station by the footbridge or underpass.
  2. Tap the Istanbulkart at the turnstile.
  3. Choose the correct platform. Signs show the final stop of each direction.
  4. Let passengers leave the bus before boarding.
  5. Follow the on-board screens and announcements to track your stop.
  6. Leave through the doors at your stop and follow the signs to the exit or transfer.

Peak hours

The busiest times are generally the weekday morning and evening commutes. At these times buses can be crowded, and travel time between stops can increase slightly. Outside these hours, vehicles are usually less full.

Accessibility

Stations and vehicles include level boarding and reserved seating areas. Seats near the doors are designated for older passengers, pregnant passengers, and passengers with disabilities.

Frequently asked questions

How many stops does the Metrobüs have?

The line has 44 stops between Beylikdüzü and Söğütlüçeşme.

Does the Metrobüs cross the Bosphorus?

Yes. It crosses on the 15 July Martyrs Bridge, using a dedicated lane, and connects the European and Asian sides of Istanbul.

Can I pay with cash?

No. Fares are paid with an Istanbulkart tapped at the station turnstile.

Who operates the Metrobüs?

İETT, the public bus operator of Istanbul, runs the line.

Does the Metrobüs connect to the metro?

Yes. Several stops, including Zeytinburnu, Mecidiyeköy, and Zincirlikuyu, link to metro or tram lines, and Söğütlüçeşme links to Marmaray.

Does the Metrobüs run at night?

The line provides late-night service with reduced frequency. Check the operator's official channels for current timetables.

Summary

The Metrobüs is a long, high-capacity bus rapid transit line that uses a dedicated lane to connect Beylikdüzü on the European side with Söğütlüçeşme on the Asian side. It has 44 stops, is operated by İETT, uses the Istanbulkart for payment, and links with several metro, tram, and rail lines. Understanding its route, its transfer points, and its payment system makes it easier to use as part of everyday travel in Istanbul.

Information is general and can change. Fares, timetables, and station details are set by the operator; check official sources for the latest data.
